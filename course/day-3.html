Cycle 1

What a Skill is, and why prose

≈ 60 min
Concept

A Skill is a file that tells Claude how to think about one specific domain. It isn't code and it isn't a settings table — it's written guidance, in plain language, that Claude loads when the work calls for it. Give Claude a Segregation of Duties Skill and it evaluates access grants the way the person who wrote that policy would.

What is Markdown?

Markdown is a lightweight way to write formatted text in a plain file — a heading is a line starting with #, bold is **stars**, a list is a run of lines starting with -. It stays readable as plain text and needs no special program to open. Skills are written in Markdown, in files that end in .md.

Why prose, not a config file? Compliance rules aren't clean if-then switches. Rules have exceptions, exceptions have conditions, and the policy language carries intent that decides the edge cases. A table can hold "Vendor Admin + AP Manager = flag." It cannot hold "unless the holder is the Controller in Finance & Accounting and the CFO's quarterly payment review is evidenced in Atlas." Prose can. That nuance is exactly what you are paid to apply — and exactly what a Skill lets Claude apply too.

Where a Skill has to live. This trips up almost everyone the first time. A project Skill is a directory containing a file called SKILL.md — not a loose .md file:

Skill layout 
.claude/skills/sod-policy/SKILL.md      <- loads
.claude/skills/sod-policy.md           <- silently ignored

Every SKILL.md opens with a short YAML block — the frontmatter — carrying two fields. name must be lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, at most 64 characters, and may not contain the words "anthropic" or "claude". description is the important one: it is the only text Claude sees when deciding whether this Skill is relevant, so it must say both what the Skill does and when to use it, written in the third person. Everything below the frontmatter is ordinary Markdown, and it loads only once the Skill is actually invoked.

SKILL.md — the shape you'll follow three times today 
---
name: some-policy-name
description: Evaluates <what> against <which policy>. Use when <the
  trigger — reviewing entitlements, triaging a grant, ...>.
---

# <Policy title, as the document names itself>

## Rule
<What the policy forbids or requires, in the policy's own terms.>

## Exemptions
<Who is excused, under what conditions, and what evidence is required.>

## How to cite
<The exact citation string a finding should carry.>

Cycle 2

Your first policy Skill — Segregation of Duties

≈ 90 min
Concept

Segregation of Duties is the principle that no single person should control a whole risky process end to end. The person who creates a vendor shouldn't also approve that vendor's payments. Meridian states the prohibition in ACP-4.1 and delegates the actual list of conflicting pairs to the SoD matrix at data/2026-Q3/sod_matrix.json, which defines six conflicts — including the Vendor Admin + AP Manager pair in Atlas ERP, rated critical, with the reason recorded as "Create a vendor, then approve its payment."

The exemption is the whole lesson. ACP-4.2 says that where business necessity requires conflicting roles, the CFO may grant a written exemption with a compensating control — and it names one standing case: the Controller in Finance & Accounting may hold both AP Manager and Vendor Admin in Atlas ERP, compensated by a quarterly manual payment review by the CFO, evidenced in Atlas. The clause closes with a sentence worth copying into your Skill nearly verbatim: access held under ACP-4.2 is not a finding.

You already know what this Skill has to reproduce, because you did it by hand yesterday. Five accounts in your ledger hold both roles in Atlas ERP. Three are Controllers and are exempt. Two are real conflicts:

A000042 Patrick Ramos Controller, Finance & Accounting -> exempt, ACP-4.2 A000089 Amanda Garrison Controller, Finance & Accounting -> exempt, ACP-4.2 A000168 April Tyler Controller, Finance & Accounting -> exempt, ACP-4.2 A000251 Michelle Mercado Senior Accountant, F&A -> FINDING A000615 James Hernandez Field Services, terminated -> FINDING

Yesterday those two findings carried whatever citation you typed. Today they carry citations a Skill produced from the policy text — and the three exemptions carry the clause that suppressed them. Same answer, defensible provenance.

Cycle 3

Two more policies

≈ 90 min
Concept

You know the rhythm now: read the policy, draft, review for the three failure modes, save to the right path, test on known cases. The second and third times should be noticeably faster — that speed-up is the skill you're building. What changes is the shape of the exceptions.

The Privileged Access Standard carries two separate requirements, and reading it carelessly collapses them into one. Privileged roles need an approved access request before they're granted, and must show activity within 180 days or be reviewed for removal. The break-glass carve-out — "break-glass accounts are exempt from the dormancy rule when registered and documented" — excuses only the second. A registered break-glass account still needs its approving ticket. Note also that the exemption is conditional: "registered and documented" means the account appears in data/2026-Q3/service_accounts.json, which is a thing to check rather than assume.

What counts as privileged isn't a guess either — applications.json lists privileged_roles per application. Across your ledger that's 105 privileged grants. Measured against quarter end, 2026-09-30, five of them have been dormant beyond 180 days:

A000365 gabriel.villa Vault / Admin 2025-12-09 295d A000605 debbie.gonzalez Vault / Admin 2025-11-29 305d A000768 robert.miller Vault / Admin 2025-09-30 365d A000963 emergency.admin Active Directory / Domain Admin 2026-01-05 268d A001015 emergency.admin Active Directory / Domain Admin 2025-10-19 346d

The two emergency.admin accounts are registered break-glass — both appear in service_accounts.json and both hold an approved ticket. The rule is one sentence: access ends on the statement-of-work end date, and an extension requires a new approved ticket referencing the renewed SOW, dated before the prior end date. Four accounts, 46 grants, and seven logins recorded after the SOW lapsed. Two details will catch a careless Skill. Jamie Alvarez holds two accounts, and both lapse with the same SOW — the second is easy to miss. Concept

  1. Update triage to apply all three Skills.

    Why: triage stops being one rule and becomes a pass over the ledger that consults each relevant policy.

    What you're building. A triage step that, for the whole Q3 ledger, evaluates SoD conflicts, privileged access, and contractor access, and emits a finding per violation in the contract shape. Keep the deterministic work deterministic: which accounts hold conflicting pairs, which grants are privileged, which contractors have lapsed — that's a join over files, and it was yesterday's lesson. The Skills supply the judgment on top: does this breach the policy, is it excused, and what does it cite.

    How you'll know you got it right. The run completes over the full ledger — all 15,081 rows in scope, not a sample — and produces findings in all three categories. If one category comes back empty, that Skill isn't reaching triage; fix that before tuning anything else.

    Suggested workflow. Describe the pipeline you want rather than the code: read the ledger, determine the candidate populations for each policy, apply the matching Skill to each candidate, and collect the results into the contract shape. Ask Claude Code to report per-category counts as it goes — those counts are how you'll catch a Skill that silently matched nothing.

  2. Populate policy_citations on every finding.

    Why: this is the day's whole point. A finding without a citation is the thing you set out today to stop producing.

    What you're building. Every finding in findings.json carrying a non-empty policy_citations array whose entries resolve to real text in data/2026-Q3/policies/. And every entry in exemptions.json naming the clause that suppressed it.

    What a good citation set looks like, given that only the ACP is numbered:

    sod_conflict ["ACP-4.1", "SoD Policy"] suppressed ["ACP-4.2"] -> exemptions.json dormant_privileged ["Privileged Access Standard"] suppressed ["Privileged Access Standard"] (break-glass carve-out) contractor_overstay ["Contractor Access Standard"]

    Cite the document title where a policy has no clause numbers. That is honest and it resolves. A fabricated "§3.1" resolves to nothing and is the one outcome worse than a blank field.

    How you'll know you got it right. Count them: the number of findings with a non-empty policy_citations equals the total number of findings. Then spot-check by hand — take one citation from each category, open the policy file, and confirm the sentence is there.

    Stuck?

    Schema validation fails on an unknown field? The contract sets additionalProperties: false. Fields like finding_id, policy_reference or status aren't in it. Use policy_citations, and put anything the contract can't hold in the exemptions sidecar.

    Some findings have an empty citation array? Ask which Skill produced them. Usually one Skill's citation instruction is vaguer than the other two's — tighten its "how to cite" section rather than patching the output.

  3. Run the full Q3 pass and verify the totals.

    Why: your proof. Every number here is knowable in advance, so any deviation is a real signal rather than a judgment call.

    How you'll know you got it right. Nine findings across three categories, five recorded exemptions, and full citation coverage:

    category findings accounts sod_conflict 2 A000251, A000615 dormant_privileged 3 A000365, A000605, A000768 contractor_overstay 4 A001143, A001163, A000175, A000577 ---- total 9 with policy_citations: 9 / 9 exemptions.json 5 A000042, A000089, A000168 (ACP-4.2) A000963, A001015 (break-glass)

    If you scoped contractor findings per grant rather than per account, your contractor count will be 46 rather than 4 — that is defensible, but say so in your notes and expect the account set to be the same four. Everything else should match exactly.

    Stuck?

    More findings than expected? Look at which category grew. An inflated dormant_privileged usually means every role is being treated as privileged; an inflated contractor_overstay usually means contractor status alone is being read as a violation.

    The Controllers are back? Yesterday's exemption regressed while you were rewiring triage. tests/test_ledger.py catches this — run it alongside today's tests.
