Build the SoD Skill and make it honor the exemption

Depends on: Sub-task 16 — you need the clause inventory and the skills directory Time: ≈ 90 min Type: building block #2
First appearance
exemption as first-class output exemptions.json

Building block two of six. After this sub-task you have a Skill that clears people as deliberately as it flags them.

You already found Meridian's two real SoD conflicts in sub-task 12, in pure code, with no model involved. That was the right way to do it — set containment against a matrix in a file is not a judgment call.

So why build a Skill for it? Because the deterministic version only worked because you read ACP-4.2 and hard-coded the carve-out. The Skill has to encode the rule and its exemption in a form a model can apply to a person it has never seen, in a quarter you haven't generated yet. That is a different and harder artifact, and it is the one that travels.

The exemption is the whole exercise. Three Controllers hold the conflicting pair legitimately. A Skill that flags them is worse than no Skill.

What you'll understand and do

  • Author a Skill that encodes a rule and its scoped carve-out
  • Make "not a finding" an output the system produces rather than a silence
  • Test it against all five holders of the conflicting pair and get 2 flagged, 3 cleared

Concept

The one idea

A suppression you can't see is indistinguishable from a miss.

ACP-4.2 closes with a sentence worth quoting exactly: "Access held under ACP-4.2 is not a finding." The policy author bothered to say that explicitly, and the reason is that a reviewer reading a clean report cannot tell the difference between we checked and it's fine and we never checked.

Exemption as a first-class output

Your system produces two artifacts, not one. findings.json holds what you are reporting. exemptions.json holds what you deliberately chose not to report and the clause that authorised the suppression.

The second file is not paperwork. It is the evidence that your review was thorough, and in an audit it is the more interesting document — anyone can produce findings. Producing defensible non-findings is the harder half.

You built this file by hand in sub-task 12. From here on the Skill decides what goes in it.

Why the exemption can't be a list of account IDs. This is the trap, and it is set now to spring in Theme I. The three exempt Controllers this quarter are A000387, A000674 and A001125. Writing those three IDs into your Skill or your code produces exactly the right answer for 2026-Q3 and a catastrophically wrong one for any other quarter — three false positives on the new Controllers and three false negatives on people who left.

The exemption is keyed on title + department + role pair + application + compensating control, because that is what ACP-4.2 says. The account IDs are a consequence, never an input. sod_matrix.json mirrors the clause structurally in its exemptions block — title, department, roles, app, clause, compensating control — which tells you the data model agrees.

What the Skill is for, given the code already works

Three things the deterministic version can't do. It can read the exemption's reasoning and apply it to a case the matrix doesn't literally cover. It can produce the rationale prose a finding needs. And it can say "this is close to the exemption but doesn't meet it, and here is which condition fails" — which is precisely what A001115 is.

Task 1

Author the Skill

  1. Create .claude/skills/sod-policy/SKILL.md with valid frontmatter.

    Why: directory, not a flat file. The name must match the directory and satisfy the character rules from sub-task 16. The description is the routing text — write it in the third person, and make it say both what the Skill covers and when it applies.

    frontmatter shape — write your own description 
    ---
name: sod-policy
description: <what this covers, and when Claude should reach for it —
             one or two sentences, third person, well over 30 characters>
---

<the body: the rule, the exemption, how to cite, what to emit>
  2. Write the body so it covers the rule, the exemption, the citation form, and the two possible outputs.

    Why: intent, not a template. A reviewer with no Meridian context should be able to read your Skill and correctly adjudicate a case you didn't anticipate. Four things have to be in there for that to be true.

    The rule. Conflicting role pairs are defined in sod_matrix.json, not in your Skill — the Skill says where to look and what a conflict means. ACP-4.1 is the clause that forbids it, and it must appear by name.

    The exemption, with all of its conditions. ACP-4.2, scoped to a Controller in Finance & Accounting holding AP Manager plus Vendor Admin in Atlas ERP, with the CFO's quarterly manual payment review as the compensating control. Every condition, or the Skill will over-apply it.

    How to cite. ACP-4.1 and ACP-4.2 by number; the SoD Policy by title only. Say explicitly that the SoD Policy has no section numbers — that one sentence is what stops sub-task 15's failure recurring.

    Both outputs. A conflict that isn't exempt is a finding. A conflict that is exempt is an exemption record naming ACP-4.2 and the compensating control. Neither case is silence.

  3. Check the Skill loads.

    Why: if you created .claude/skills/ this session rather than in sub-task 16, restart first. A Skill that isn't discovered fails silently, which is the worst possible failure mode to debug from output alone.

Stuck?

How long should the body be? Long enough to adjudicate an unanticipated case; short enough that every sentence earns its place. A few hundred words is normal for a policy this size. If you're writing paragraphs the model would already know without you, cut them.

Should I paste the whole policy text in? Quoting the operative sentences is good — especially ACP-4.2's closing line, which is doing real work. Pasting all five documents into one Skill defeats progressive disclosure; you'd be back to paying for everything on every call.

My description is one line and feels thin. Then it is. The tests reject descriptions under about 30 characters for a reason: that string is the entire basis on which Claude decides whether to load your Skill. Say what it covers and when it applies.

Task 2

Test it on all five holders — both outcomes

  1. Run the sub-task 14 agent, with the Skill available, over each of the five accounts holding Atlas ERP Vendor Admin + AP Manager.

    Why: five cases, two outcomes, and the split is the test. Every Vendor Admin grant in the company is also an AP Manager — five out of five, which you found in sub-task 12.

    expected split 
    FINDING   A000384  james.hernandez   E00023  Ranger/warden
                   Field Services      terminated 2026-07-07
FINDING   A001115  michelle.mercado  E00251  Senior Accountant
                   Finance & Accounting  active

EXEMPT    A000387  april.tyler       E00168  Controller  F&A
EXEMPT    A000674  patrick.ramos     E00042  Controller  F&A
EXEMPT    A001125  amanda.garrison   E00089  Controller  F&A
  2. Read the reasoning on the two that stayed findings, and check it names the right condition.

    Why: both are near-misses on the exemption, and for different reasons. A001115 is a Senior Accountant in Finance & Accounting — right department, wrong title. If the rationale says "not in Finance & Accounting" the Skill got the right answer for the wrong reason, which will fail on the next quarter. A000384 is a Ranger/warden in Field Services who was terminated on 2026-07-07 and still holds both roles in the finance system; the exemption is nowhere near them, and the termination is arguably the more alarming fact.

  3. Check the three exemption records carry the clause and the compensating control.

    Why: "suppressed" is not a reason. Each record needs the account, the clause — ACP-4.2 — and the compensating control it rests on, the CFO's quarterly manual payment review. A reviewer must be able to challenge the suppression on its merits, which requires knowing what those merits were.

  4. Verify every citation against your sub-task 16 inventory.

    Why: this is the first payoff. Take each policy_citations entry and check it appears in your list. ACP-4.1 and ACP-4.2 are real. "SoD Policy" is real. "SoD Policy §2.3" is not, and now you catch it in four seconds instead of shipping it.

Stuck?

It flagged the Controllers. The Skill states the exemption but doesn't make it operative — usually because the rule reads as absolute and the exemption as a footnote. Try stating both conditions as a single test the model has to run: is this pair conflicting and not covered by ACP-4.2?

It exempted A001115 too. The opposite failure, and worse: a real conflict cleared. The exemption needs both title Controller and department Finance & Accounting. Senior Accountant is not Controller, however senior. Make the conjunction explicit in the Skill.

It exempted A000384 because it's not in Finance. Read that again — being outside Finance means the exemption doesn't apply, so the conflict stands. If the Skill is treating "outside the exemption's scope" as "outside the rule's scope", the rule and the carve-out have been collapsed into one condition.

Is it cheating to also run the deterministic version? No — that's a regression test, and running both is exactly right. You know the answer from sub-task 12; the Skill has to reach it by reasoning rather than by set arithmetic, and comparing the two is how you find out whether it did.
