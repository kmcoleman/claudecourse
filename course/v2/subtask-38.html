Hunt every place you memorized one quarter

Depends on: Sub-task 37 — you need the Q4 export and the list of Q3 references Time: ≈ 90 min Type: generalization · overfitting
First appearance
overfitting derived vs hardcoded parameters

Memorization has a shape. Four shapes, in fact, and every one of them is in your repo somewhere.

The split-brain configuration was one bug with one fix. What sub-task 37's grep turned up is the real work: every place where a Q3 specific found its way into something that should have been general.

These are not sloppy mistakes. Every one of them was the shortest correct path at the time you wrote it. Hardcoding three Controller account IDs is faster than implementing a title-and- department test, and it produces exactly the right answer for the quarter in front of you.

Memorization has a shape, and once you can see the shape you find it everywhere.

What you'll understand and do

  • Recognize the four shapes of memorization: IDs, counts, dates, paths
  • Fix each one by deriving the parameter rather than stating it
  • Produce a trap-by-trap fix log
  • Get a correct Q4 run — eight findings, five exemptions

Concept

The one idea

Derive it, or the next quarter derives it for you.

Every parameter in your system is either derived from the data being reviewed or stated by you. Stated parameters are faster to write, easier to read, and correct exactly once.

The four shapes

Hardcoded identifiers. Account IDs, employee IDs, names. The worst, because they fail in both directions: the Q3 entities aren't in Q4 (so exemptions don't apply and you get false positives) and the Q4 entities aren't recognised (so you get the same false positives again from the other side).

Hardcoded counts. assert len(findings) == 9. Q4 has eight. A test that encodes an answer rather than a property fails on the first correct run against new data, which is the worst possible time for a test to cry wolf.

Hardcoded dates. An as-of date of 2026-09-30, a dormancy cutoff of 2026-04-03. Q4 ends 2026-12-31. A contractor whose SOW ended in December looks perfectly current against a September as-of date, and the overstay vanishes silently.

Hardcoded paths. data/2026-Q3 anywhere — in .mcp.json, in a default argument, in a hook's environment variable.

Why Q4 has eight findings and not nine. This is the specific number that catches people, and the reason is worth understanding rather than memorizing.

In Q3, four accounts were contractor overstays but only three people: Jamie Alvarez held two accounts, and both lapsed with the same SOW. In Q4 the three expired contractors hold one account each. Same rule, same policy, different population, different count — 2 SoD + 3 dormant + 3 contractor = 8.

So an assertion on the number nine is not a slightly-wrong test. It is a test that has encoded an accident of one quarter's account provisioning as though it were a property of the control.

What to key on instead

The ACP-4.2 exemption → title is Controller and department is Finance & Accounting and the role pair and application match and a compensating control is documented. Never an account ID.

Break-glass → the account name appears in service_accounts.json. emergency.admin is seed-stable; the account IDs holding it are not.

The as-of date → derived from the quarter under review. Q3 ends 2026-09-30; Q4 ends 2026-12-31. The dormancy cutoff is as-of minus 180 days, computed rather than typed.

Counts → assert properties, not totals. Every finding validates. Every citation is real. No account appears twice in a category. All of those are true on every quarter.

Do this

Task 1

Find them all before fixing any

  1. Sweep the repo for all four shapes.

    Why: build the complete list first. Fixing as you go means you stop when the run turns green, which is usually two traps before the end.

    terminal 
    grep -rn "A00[0-9]\{4\}\|E0[0-9]\{4\}" --include="*.py" --include="*.md" --include="*.json" . \
  | grep -v "^./data/"
grep -rn "2026-Q3\|2026-09-30\|2026-04-03" . | grep -v "^./data/"
grep -rn "== 9\|== 5\|len(findings)" --include="*.py" .

    Search your Skills too. .claude/skills/ is prose, and a Skill that says "the exempt Controllers are A000387, A000674 and A001125" is exactly as hardcoded as a Python list, and much easier to miss.

  2. Check the two the kit itself ships.

    Why: not all the traps are yours. .mcp.json pins MERIDIAN_DATA_DIR to data/2026-Q3 — you found that in sub-task 37. And audit_write.py defaults MERIDIAN_QUARTER to 2026-Q3, which means your Q4 audit trail lands in the Q3 log unless you set it. You flagged that one yourself in sub-task 27.

    Both are realistic. Real systems are full of configuration defaults that were correct on the day someone wrote them.

  3. Classify each finding by shape and predict its failure.

    Why: predicting first is what turns a fix list into understanding. For each entry: which shape is it, and what specifically goes wrong on Q4? A hardcoded Controller list produces false positives on Q4's real Controllers. A hardcoded as-of date hides December contractor overstays. Write the prediction, then verify it.

Task 2

Fix by deriving

  1. Key the ACP-4.2 exemption on title and department.

    Why: the highest-impact fix. Q4 has three Controllers in Finance & Accounting holding the conflicting pair, and an ID-keyed exemption recognises none of them — so SoD findings jump from two to five, all three extras being false positives on people the policy explicitly exempts.

    You may already have done this in sub-task 17, where the worksheet asked how your Skill decides the exemption applies. If so, verify it rather than assuming. Skills drift.

  2. Key break-glass on the account name, not the ID.

    Why: Q3's break-glass accounts were A001159 and A001199. Q4's are different IDs entirely. What is stable is the name — emergency.admin — and the fact that it appears in service_accounts.json. Check registration against the file, which is what the Privileged Access Standard actually asks for.

  3. Derive the as-of date from the quarter.

    Why: the subtlest trap and the one most likely to still be lurking. Parse the quarter from export_dir and compute its end date — Q3 ends 2026-09-30, Q4 ends 2026-12-31 — then derive the dormancy cutoff as 180 days before it.

    Q3 hid this. Its three dormant admins were stale by such a margin that the exact cutoff didn't matter, so a wrong as-of date produced the right answer. Q4 does not hide it: the contractors' term dates fall in December, days before quarter end. Evaluate them against a September as-of date and they aren't terminated yet, and three findings disappear without a sound.

  4. Replace count assertions with property assertions.

    Why: delete every == 9 and == 5. Replace with things that are true of any correct run: every finding validates, every citation is one of the ten legitimate identifiers, no account appears twice in a category, every suppression is recorded in the exemptions file, and every account_id exists in the export under review.

    That last one is the provenance check you added in sub-task 37, and it's the single most valuable quarter-independent assertion you have.

  5. Parameterize every path, including the two the kit ships.

    Why: MERIDIAN_DATA_DIR and MERIDIAN_QUARTER should both follow whatever quarter you're reviewing. The cleanest arrangement is a single place that derives every quarter-dependent value from export_dir, so the two halves of the system cannot disagree the way they did in sub-task 37.

  6. Re-run Q4 and check against ground truth.

    Why: now you get the numbers. Eight findings, five exemptions, and a completely different cast from Q3.

    2026-Q4 — 8 findings, 5 exemptions 
    sod_conflict          2   A000276  A000523
dormant_privileged    3   A000690  A000820  A000879     (all Vault/Admin)
contractor_overstay   3   A000133  A000157  A000749

exemptions            5   A000518  A000895  A001168     (ACP-4.2 Controllers)
                          A000504  A001045              (break-glass)

    Three contractor findings, not four — because no contractor in this quarter holds a second account. If you get four, check for a duplicate. If you get zero, your as-of date is still wrong.

  7. Write the fix log.
    Trap-by-trap fix log

    One row per trap. This is the checklist you take to the next engagement, and it's more valuable than the code.

    Saved in this browser
  8. Commit.

    Every fix is a generalization. Say so in the message — the diff shows what changed, not why it was fragile.

Stuck?

Five SoD findings instead of two. The ACP-4.2 exemption isn't reaching Q4's Controllers. Check whether it's keyed on account IDs, and check the Skill's prose as well as the code — a Skill that names the Q3 Controllers by ID will happily flag the Q4 ones.

Five dormant findings instead of three. Break-glass isn't recognised. Q4's are different account IDs; check the account name against service_accounts.json.

Zero contractor findings. The as-of date. Q4's contractors terminate in December, so against a September as-of they aren't terminated yet. Derive the date.

A test fails saying it expected 9. That's a count assertion doing exactly what count assertions do. Q4 has eight. Replace it with a property.

My audit log is empty for Q4. MERIDIAN_QUARTER still defaults to 2026-Q3, so the Q4 writes went into the Q3 log. Set it, or derive it from the quarter under review.

You're done when
  • Every instance of all four shapes found, classified, and fixed by deriving
  • The ACP-4.2 exemption keyed on title and department; break-glass on account name
  • The as-of date and dormancy cutoff derived from the quarter under review
  • Count assertions replaced with quarter-independent properties
  • A correct Q4 run: 8 findings (2 SoD, 3 dormant, 3 contractor) and 5 exemptions
  • A trap-by-trap fix log you could hand to someone else

The system now derives what it used to state. Whether that's actually true is not something you get to assert — sub-task 39 makes you demonstrate it.

If you're curious

  • Generate a third quarter under a seed nobody has told you the answers for, and run it. You have no ground truth, which means the only things you can check are the quarter-independent properties you just wrote. That is the real test, and it's the situation every client engagement puts you in.
  • Re-read your Skills as if you'd never seen Meridian. Anywhere they mention a specific person, ID or count is a place where a policy document has been contaminated with an answer key.
