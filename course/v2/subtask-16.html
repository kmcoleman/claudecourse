Inventory what the five policies really say

Depends on: Sub-task 15 — you need the fabricated citation to compare against Time: ≈ 60 min Type: skills · grounding
First appearance
Skills SKILL.md frontmatter progressive disclosure

Building block two starts here. This sub-task is the reading; sub-task 17 is the writing.

Sub-task 15 left you with a fabricated citation and a diagnosis: the model has never read Meridian's policies. The obvious next move is to hand them over. Before you can do that well, you have to know what is actually in them — and the answer is genuinely surprising.

Five documents. Under 2,000 words total. Exactly five numbered clauses across the whole set, all in one file. Everything else has to be cited by document title because there is nothing else to cite.

An hour spent building that inventory is an hour that makes fabricated citations structurally impossible for the rest of the course.

What you'll understand and do

  • Read all five policy documents and extract every citable clause
  • Explain what a Skill is, where it lives on disk, and why the format is strict
  • Say why a Skill is prose rather than a config table
  • Produce a clause inventory you can check any citation against in seconds

Concept

The one idea

A Skill is a document you hand the model, loaded only when it's relevant.

Rules — building block one, from sub-task 7 — are standing instructions read at the start of every session. They are always in context and they cost you every turn. That's the right trade for "never change the contract signature." It's the wrong trade for a policy that matters on one finding in ten.

What is a Skill?

A directory under .claude/skills/ containing a file called SKILL.md. The file opens with YAML frontmatter carrying a name and a description; everything after it is the body.

.claude/skills/sod-policy/SKILL.md

Only the description is always in context. Claude reads the descriptions of every available Skill, decides which are relevant to the task at hand, and loads the full body only for those. That is progressive disclosure, and it is why Skills scale where pasting documents into the prompt doesn't.

The description is therefore the most important line in the file. It is the only text Claude sees when deciding whether to load the Skill. It must say what the Skill does and when to apply it.

Two format facts that will cost you an hour if you miss them

A Skill is a directory, not a file. .claude/skills/sod-policy.md is silently ignored — no error, no warning, it simply never loads. It must be .claude/skills/sod-policy/SKILL.md.

Creating the top-level .claude/skills/ directory mid-session requires restarting Claude Code before the first Skill is discovered. Make the directory now, before you need it, and save yourself the debugging.

name must be lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, at most 64 characters, and may not contain "claude" or "anthropic". description is capped at 1,024 characters — and the kit's tests reject anything under about 30, because a description too thin to route on is a Skill that never loads.

Why prose and not a config table. The temptation is to reduce each policy to structured rules: {"dormancy_days": 180, "exempt": ["break-glass"]}. Resist it, for a reason that is about honesty rather than style.

A config table forces you to resolve every ambiguity at authoring time, silently, on your own judgment. The Contractor Access Standard requires "a new approved ticket referencing the renewed SOW before the prior end date." Reduce that to a boolean and you have made three interpretive decisions and left no trace of any of them. Keep it as prose and the ambiguity stays visible — to the model, which can flag it, and to the reviewer, who can argue with it. Sub-task 18 is where that distinction stops being philosophical and costs you four real findings.

Task 1

Read all five, properly

  1. Read every file in data/2026-Q3/policies/, end to end.

    Why: they are 157 to 811 bytes each. The whole set takes ten minutes and you will be authoring against them for the next three sub-tasks. Skimming here is how sub-task 18's trap catches you.

    terminal 
    wc -c data/2026-Q3/policies/*.md
for f in data/2026-Q3/policies/*.md; do echo "== $f"; cat "$f"; done
  2. Enumerate every clause identifier in the entire policy set.

    Why: you ran a version of this in sub-task 15 to disprove one citation. Run it again as a positive inventory — this is the complete list of things that can legitimately be cited by number, and there are five of them.

    expected output 
    access-control-policy.md   ACP-1    Purpose
                           ACP-4    Segregation of Duties
                           ACP-4.1  conflicting duties must not be held by one person
                           ACP-4.2  documented exemption (the Controller carve-out)
                           ACP-6    Termination

sod-policy.md                (no clause numbers — cite by title)
privileged-access-standard.md (no clause numbers — cite by title)
contractor-access-standard.md (no clause numbers — cite by title)

    Four documents out of five have no internal numbering at all. Any citation of the form "SoD Policy §2.3" or "Privileged Access Standard §3.2" is fabricated by construction — there is no section to number. That single fact is the check you will apply for the rest of the course.

Task 2

Write down what each document requires — in its own words

  1. For each of the five, record: what it requires, what it exempts, and how it must be cited.

    Why: the exemptions are the part people skip, and they are where all the false positives live. Every one of these documents that has a carve-out has a scoped carve-out, and the scope is load-bearing.

    the five, condensed — verify each against the file 
    Access Control Policy
  ACP-4.1  conflicting duties from the SoD matrix, one individual: forbidden
  ACP-4.2  standing exemption. Controller, Finance & Accounting, may hold
           AP Manager + Vendor Admin in Atlas ERP. Compensating control:
           quarterly manual payment review by the CFO. Closes with:
           "Access held under ACP-4.2 is not a finding."
  ACP-6    access revoked on the employee's termination date

SoD Policy                    (cite by title)
  conflicts are findings unless covered by a documented ACP-4.2 exemption

Privileged Access Standard    (cite by title)
  TWO requirements: an approved ticket BEFORE grant, AND activity within
  180 days. Break-glass accounts are exempt from the dormancy rule ONLY,
  and only "when registered and documented".

Contractor Access Standard    (cite by title)
  access ends on the SOW end date. Extension needs a NEW approved ticket
  REFERENCING THE RENEWED SOW, dated BEFORE the prior end date.

Termination Procedure         (cite by title)
  all access revoked within 24 hours of the termination date.
  "Employees on approved leave retain access; leave is not termination."
  2. Mark the three phrases that will decide findings, and say why.

    Why: each of these is a scope limit that a careless reading drops, and dropping it changes the answer.

    "Break-glass accounts are exempt from the dormancy rule." From the dormancy rule — not from the ticket requirement. A break-glass account with no approving ticket is still a finding on that half.

    "referencing the renewed SOW." Not "a ticket". Not "an approved ticket". A ticket that references the renewed statement of work. Sub-task 18 is built entirely on this clause.

    "leave is not termination." A pure false-positive guard. Four employees are on_leave and none of them is a termination finding.

  3. Create the skills directory now, empty, and restart Claude Code.

    Why: the watcher quirk. Creating .claude/skills/ during a session means the first Skill you write isn't discovered until you restart. Doing it now costs nothing; doing it in the middle of sub-task 17 costs you twenty minutes of believing your Skill is broken.

    terminal 
    mkdir -p .claude/skills
# then restart Claude Code before creating your first SKILL.md
  4. Record the inventory somewhere you'll actually use it.

    Why: this is your citation checker. Every time an agent produces a policy_citations entry from here on, you check it against this list. If it isn't one of the five clause numbers or one of the five document titles, it's fabricated.

Stuck?

These policies are really short. Is that realistic? Shorter than a real utility's, yes. Not less ambiguous — that's the part that's true to life. Real policy sets are longer and no clearer, and the reading discipline you build on 2,000 words is the same one you'd apply to 200 pages.

Should the Termination Procedure and Access Control Policy be one Skill? A judgment call, and defensible either way. The kit's tests require three specific Skills — SoD, privileged access, contractor access — and are silent on the other two. Termination logic is small enough to fold into the Skill that needs it; if you split it out, give it a description that clearly distinguishes it from the others, or Claude will load the wrong one.

You're done when
  • All five policy documents read end to end
  • A clause inventory listing exactly five citable clause numbers, all from access-control-policy.md, and four documents confirmed to have none
  • Each document's requirement and its exemption written down, with the exemption's scope stated
  • .claude/skills/ created and the session restarted

You can now check any citation in seconds. That is what makes a fabricated citation impossible to slip past you — not vigilance, a list.
