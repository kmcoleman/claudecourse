The last sub-task, and the last building block. Generalization is a demonstrated property, never a claim.

You fixed the traps and Q4 came out right. That is not yet a demonstration of anything, for a reason worth taking seriously: you fixed Q4 while looking at Q4's answers, which is the same activity that produced the memorization in the first place.

The claim you actually want to make is stronger. One codebase, unmodified, produces correct results on both quarters, and every evaluation passes on both.

That claim you can only make by running it.

What you'll understand and do

  • Run both quarters from one unmodified codebase

Concept

The one idea

Generalization is a property you demonstrate, not one you claim.

"The system generalizes" is a hypothesis. Every project believes it right up until someone runs the second dataset, and it is not an unreasonable belief — it's just not evidence.

The evidence is boring and specific: the same commit, run twice, against two exports, producing two correct answers, with every check green on both. Nothing in between. No branch, no environment variable set differently for one of them, no "well, on Q4 we also do this".

Why Q3 has to be re-run

This is the half people skip, and it is where the interesting failure lives.

You spent sub-task 38 changing exemption logic, date derivation, path handling and test assertions. Every one of those touched the code path that produces Q3's answers. It is entirely possible — common, actually — to generalize a system in a way that breaks the case it started from.

A fix that makes Q4 right and Q3 wrong hasn't generalized anything. It has moved the memorization.

Two quarters is a demonstration, not a proof. Both were generated by the same generator from the same world model, so they share more structure than two real clients would. Your system has demonstrated that it doesn't depend on one population. It has not demonstrated independence from Meridian's schema, its policy structure, or its 22 applications.

Being precise about that is the difference between a claim you can defend and one that falls apart in the first real engagement. What you can say is: this system's behaviour depends on the policies and the data, not on the specific people in it, and here are two quarters demonstrating that.

Stuck?

Q4 is right and Q3 regressed. The most instructive possible outcome and worth every minute it takes to fix. Score Q3 against the micro-key to find which accounts moved, then look at what changed in sub-task 38 that touches them. The as-of derivation is the usual culprit — an off-by-one on quarter end shifts the dormancy cutoff.

One run needs an environment variable set by hand. Then it isn't derived. Move the derivation into the code so export_dir determines every quarter-dependent value.

test_transfer.py fails on the account sets. Check the seed first — anything other than 20261015 produces a valid quarter with different answers. If the seed is right, the failure names which category is off, and sub-task 38's Stuck section maps each one to its trap.

Everything passes. Am I actually done? Yes. Both quarters correct from one commit, every eval green, the enforcement layer travelling, and the full suite including test_transfer.py. That is the deliverable.
